\section{Choosing the decision model: Jev is the best price--quality choice}\label{sec:choose}

\textbf{Claim.} Among the system-1 models we tested, Jev is the best price--quality tradeoff when cost and latency
matter: it is the cheapest and fastest accurate judge, and no dearer model was shown more accurate in the preregistered
test. OpenAI's Decisions API is the main challenger (post hoc: the same or higher accuracy at
\OaPriceXLo--\OaPriceXHi\X\ the price, with fewer automated real reads).

\paragraph{Evidence} On the preregistered constructed holdout, Jev answered \JevHoldAccK\ of \HoldN\ cases correctly at
\$\JevHoldCost\ per million decisions with a client p95 of \JevHoldPninetyfiveRange\,ms (\cref{fig:frontier}). GPT-6
Luna answered \LunaHoldAccK\ at \LunaHoldCostX\X\ the cost and a p95 of \LunaHoldPninetyfiveRangeS\,s; GPT-6.1 Sol
answered \SolHoldAccK\ at \SolHoldCostX\X\ the cost and \SolHoldPninetyfiveRangeS\,s. That is an apparent accuracy
premium of 8--11 points (\LunaHoldRAccDiff\ and \SolHoldRAccDiff; \LunaHoldRAccCandOnly\ and \SolHoldRAccCandOnly\ cases,
never wrong where Jev was right), with paired bootstrap intervals of \LunaHoldRAccDiffCI\ and \SolHoldRAccDiffCI\ points,
that the corrected preregistered tests could not confirm (Holm $p$ = \LunaHoldRAccPHolm\ and \SolHoldRAccPHolm, over a
13-arm family) \prereg. Clef scored \ClHoldClefAcc\ against an in-run Jev's \ClHoldJevAcc\ at
\ClClefCostXLow--\ClClefCostXHigh\X\ the cost \posthoc; Clef-Flash scored \ClHoldFlashAcc. The seven local models were
less accurate and none met the preregistered safety bar. No challenger met the preregistered rule for replacing the
incumbent (better accuracy or fewer misfires, p95 within 500\,ms, at most twice the cost).

\paragraph{The Decisions API, post hoc} OpenAI's native typed-decision endpoint was run after the preregistered work,
paired in time with an in-run Jev reference. It scored \OaDevLunaK\ against \OaDevJevK\ development cases,
\OaHoldoutLunaK\ against \OaHoldoutJevK\ holdout cases, \OaTraceDevLunaK\ against \OaTraceDevJevK\ and
\OaTraceHoldoutLunaK\ against \OaTraceHoldoutJevK\ real read decisions; only the last difference survived Holm
correction ($p = \OaTrHoldHolm$, exploratory). On real read decisions it automated fewer (\OaTrHoldLunaCovK\ against
\OaTrHoldJevCovK\ of \OaTrHoldN), so part of its accuracy gain is more fallbacks to the host. Its misfire rate was never
higher than Jev's and it cost \OaPriceXLo--\OaPriceXHi\X\ as much per decision. Its servers answered in
\OaServerPninetyfiveLo--\OaServerPninetyfiveHi\,ms at p95, but on a network that was slow for both judges the client saw
seconds; \OaInvalid\ of its \OaRequests\ requests and \OaInvalidJev\ of Jev's failed in transport.

\paragraph{On real read decisions} On \TrN\ held-out read decisions, \TrNUseful\ of \NumBase\ judges met the
preregistered usefulness rule; Jev took the most correct automatic reads (\TrJevReads\ of \TrReadCases) but also made
\TrJevWaK\ wrong ones. No model was safe enough to act alone there.

\begin{keyidea}[title={For practitioners}]
Use Jev as the default: it is the cheapest accurate option and fast enough for interactive use on a clean network path.
Pay for a dearer judge only where 8--11 points of possible accuracy are worth many times the price, and re-test the
Decisions API, which matched or beat Jev on our cases at \OaPriceXLo--\OaPriceXHi\X\ the price. Keep a fallback to the
host on real read decisions.
\end{keyidea}
