\section{Raising the difficulty: whole sessions}\label{sec:paired}

The decision that moves money is the session's starting model. To measure it we ran whole multi-turn sessions, in
pairs: the same scripted task, started together from the same frozen workspace, once on the plain host and once under
a routing policy, each with its own cache key. The main campaign (main-v1) ran \NSessions\ sessions on \NScenarios\
scenarios of \MinTurns\ to \MaxTurns\ turns, with Fable~5.1 and Opus~5.5 as hosts. Hypotheses, thresholds and the
split were preregistered; the estimator was written after the data, which the companion report states plainly.

\paragraph{Why caching made this necessary.} One of us (DK) asked whether routing still saves money in longer chats.
Prompt caching makes re-sending a conversation cheap on the model that cached it, and switching models throws that
cache away. An earlier survey had found the shipped router's Opus-host ratio going from \CaOpusRoutedOne\X\ in
single-turn sessions to \CaOpusRoutedFour\X\ in \CaTurns-turn ones. Estimates from receipts could not settle it.

\paragraph{What it found.} With Fable as host, choosing Sonnet once at the start of the session (\term{sticky})
cost \CfFableStickyPairTwo\X\ the plain host (95\,\% CI \CfFableStickyPairCITwo; a typical per-session saving of
\CfFableStickySaving\,\% as a geometric mean, and an actual spend cut of \RvSpendCutAll\,\% over all scenarios). Quality
stayed inside the preregistered margin on the test split only; over all scenarios the margin was not shown
(\QAllFableSticky, CI \QAllFableStickyCI), and \RgNTaskFail\ task types lost more (\RgTaskFailList). With Opus as host,
every routing arm cost more (\CfOpusStickyPairTwo--\CfOpusSonnetPairTwo\X): Opus's cache reads are cheap enough that
leaving them costs more than Sonnet saves. That conclusion depends on the price table; Opus routing would break
even if its cache reads cost about \$\BEStickyPair\ per million tokens instead of \$\PriceOpusRead.

\paragraph{The effort confound.} An external peer reviewer noticed that routed Sonnet ran at medium reasoning effort
while the plain-Sonnet control ran at the provider default. Two follow-ups measured effort directly. Plain Sonnet at
medium cost \EcRatio\X\ its default (\EcCI; designed in advance, provenance-limited), and plain Fable at medium cost
\EfRatio\X\ its default (\EfCI; preregistered), both with no detectable turn-pass loss on their \EfScen\ scenarios. Medium effort
independently reduced Fable cost by about \EfCutPct\,\% in the follow-up; routing remained the larger cost lever: sticky-on-Sonnet cost about \EfStickyVsMedium\X\ what Fable at medium did (exploratory, not concurrent).
The two review rounds and their responses are recorded with the companion report.

\begin{keyidea}
Measured, not estimated: on Fable, deciding once to run the session on Sonnet saved about half the cost, with a
quality caveat on some task types. On Opus it cost more. Lowering reasoning effort is a second, smaller lever that
works on both Sonnet and Fable.
\end{keyidea}
