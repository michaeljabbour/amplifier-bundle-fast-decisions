\section{Summary}\label{sec:summary}

A coding agent such as Amplifier runs on a \term{host model}: the large model that answers each turn of a
conversation. The fast-decisions bundle can \term{route} some of the work to a cheaper model (here Sonnet 5)
when it judges the work easy enough. Routing looks like an obvious saving. It is not obvious once a
conversation gets long, because of \term{prompt caching}: the provider keeps a cheap-to-reuse copy of the
conversation \emph{for each model separately}, so switching models means paying again to cache the whole
history (\cref{sec:caching}).

This report answers one question with measurements rather than estimates: \emph{what does routing an agent
to a cheaper model actually cost, in money and in quality, over realistic multi-turn sessions?}

\begin{keyidea}
\textbf{What we found} (test split; hypotheses and thresholds preregistered, estimator fixed after the data;
prices of \cref{tab:prices};
\cref{sec:confirm}):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item \textbf{Fable 5.1 host: Sonnet is cheaper.} Choosing the model once at the start of the session
    (\term{sticky}) cost \CfFableStickyPairTwo\X\ the plain host (95\,\% CI \CfFableStickyPairCITwo), a
    typical per-session saving of about \CfFableStickySaving\,\% (geometric mean of per-pair cost ratios). In
    spend, the plain Fable sessions cost \$\RvSpendAnchor\ on average and sticky ones \$\RvSpendSticky, a
    reduction of \RvSpendCutAll\,\% over all \NScenarios\ scenarios (\RvSpendCutTest\,\% on the test split).
    Turn-pass fraction was inside the preregistered margin on the test scenarios only. The shipped router, which re-chooses its model at the start of each turn, cost
    \CfFableShippedPairTwo\X; plain Sonnet for the whole session cost \CfFableSonnetPairTwo\X.
  \item \textbf{Opus 5.5 host: routing to Sonnet cost more.} Sticky \CfOpusStickyPairTwo\X, shipped
    \CfOpusShippedPairTwo\X, plain Sonnet \CfOpusSonnetPairTwo\X\ (pair reading); without the quality filter
    (arm reading) \CfOpusStickyArmTwo\X, \CfOpusShippedArmTwo\X\ and \CfOpusSonnetArmTwo\X, that is
    \CfOpusStickyArmExtra--\CfOpusSonnetArmExtra\,\% more. This depends on Opus's cache-read price: at about
    \$\BEStickyPair\ per million tokens instead of \$\PriceOpusRead, sticky would break even
    (\cref{sec:breakeven}).
  \item \textbf{Sticky cost less than shipped on both hosts:} \HThreeFable\X\ on Fable and \HThreeOpus\X\ on
    Opus. The preregistered test was only that sticky costs no more than shipped; on Fable the margin is small
    (upper bound \HThreeFableUpper).
  \item \textbf{The savings model passes a pooled check.} Fitted on \NTrain\ training scenarios, it
    predicted the held-out total within its 90\,\% interval and its intervals covered the held-out pairs at
    the preregistered rate. Its per-host and per-task figures are not reliable (\cref{sec:modellimits}).
  \item \textbf{Cloudflare Clef (post-hoc) does not replace Jev.} Added after the preregistered results, Clef matched
    Jev's accuracy on the judge splits but cost \ClClefCostXLow--\ClClefCostXHigh\X\ as much per decision with a p95 of
    \ClClefPninetyfiveLow--\ClClefPninetyfiveHigh\,ms from this machine; Clef-Flash was cheaper but less accurate
    (\cref{sec:clef}).
\end{itemize}
\end{keyidea}

The preregistration fixed the four hypotheses, their thresholds, the split and the decision rule; the
estimator, the quality filter and which cells count as savings claims were fixed after the data were collected
(\cref{sec:design}). Under that estimator all \NHypConfirmed\ hypotheses were confirmed, under both readings of
``non-inferior'' quality (\cref{sec:confirm}). Quality was tested only for cells whose cost interval shows a
saving; Opus sticky, which saves nothing, did not show non-inferior quality (\QOpusSticky, 95\,\% CI
\QOpusStickyCI). If every routed cell had to pass the quality test, \RvNHypAllQuality\ of 4 hypotheses would be
confirmed. Four cautions travel with the result.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Quality is close to the line.} Sticky on Fable passed the 5-point turn-pass margin on the test
    scenarios by \QFableStickyMarginGap; across all \QAllScen\ scenarios the margin was not shown
    (\QAllFableSticky, 95\,\% CI \QAllFableStickyCI), and routed Fable arms passed the final hidden tests
    \FinalGapLow--\FinalGapHigh\ points less often (exploratory; \cref{sec:finalpass}).
  \item \textbf{Part of the routing saving is an effort setting.} When the sticky router chose Sonnet it ran it at
    medium reasoning effort, while the plain-Sonnet control used the provider default. Those sticky sessions cost
    \RvEffFableAll\X\ (Fable) and \RvEffOpusAll\X\ (Opus) the control on the same scenarios (\cref{sec:effort}).
    An earlier draft's claim that the router adds little beyond ``use Sonnet'' is withdrawn.
  \item \textbf{Scope.} Sessions are scripted, at most \MaxTurns\ turns long, on one provider and one price
    table (\cref{sec:limits}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{How to read this report.} The report follows the whole study arc in four parts.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Part~\ref{part:judges}, fast judges.} What a decision judge is, how \NumBase\ judges did on
    constructed cases (\cref{sec:judges}) and on real decisions rebuilt from agent traces (\cref{sec:traces}),
    and the four bundle defects those studies found and fixed.
  \item \textbf{Part~\ref{part:caching}, what caching does to savings.} Why estimates were not enough
    (\cref{sec:why}), what an earlier caching survey showed (\cref{sec:survey}), and how prompt caching works,
    with a direct probe of the provider (\cref{sec:caching}).
  \item \textbf{Part~\ref{part:measuring}, measuring savings.} How the design evolved and how big it had to be
    (\cref{sec:design}), how it was run safely (\cref{sec:running}), the confirmatory results
    (\cref{sec:confirm}) and the exploratory ones (\cref{sec:explore,sec:more}), labelled as such.
  \item \textbf{Part~\ref{part:implications}.} Recommendations, limitations and how to reproduce every number
    (\cref{sec:recs,sec:limits,sec:repro}). A glossary is in \cref{app:glossary}.
\end{itemize}
Results marked \prereg\ test hypotheses fixed in advance on held-out data; results marked \screen\ are exploratory.
Every figure has a ``How to read this figure'' note.
